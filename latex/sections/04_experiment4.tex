\section{Experiment 4: Are Bit Tricks Really Faster? (Compiler Optimization \& Two's Complement)}

\subsection{Aim}
To examine whether bit shifts and bitwise masks execute faster than division and modulo operations, identify the conditions under which compiler strength reduction renders such manual micro-optimizations redundant, and uncover semantic discrepancies when processing negative numbers in two's complement representation.

\subsection{Background}
Programmers frequently substitute power-of-two division and modulo operations with bitwise operations:
\begin{equation}
    x / 8 \stackrel{?}{\iff} x \gg 3, \qquad x \pmod{16} \stackrel{?}{\iff} x \ \& \ 15
\end{equation}
Modern optimizing compilers (\texttt{xtensa-esp32-elf-gcc}) automatically perform \textit{strength reduction}, substituting constant divisions with shift and mask instructions. However, if the divisor is stored in a runtime variable (or declared \texttt{volatile}), the compiler is precluded from assuming a constant power-of-two and must emit a full hardware division instruction (\texttt{quos}). Furthermore, in the ISO C standard, integer division \textbf{truncates toward zero}, whereas an arithmetic right shift on a signed integer performs a \textbf{floor toward negative infinity} ($\lfloor \cdot \rfloor$).

\subsection{Method}
We executed unrolled loops of 100 operations across 10 trials comparing:
\begin{enumerate}[noitemsep]
    \item Constant literal division ($x / 8$) vs. Arithmetic right shift ($x \gg 3$) vs. Variable division ($x / \text{div}$).
    \item Constant modulo ($x \pmod{16}$) vs. Bitwise AND ($x \ \& \ 15$) vs. Variable modulo ($x \pmod{\text{mod}}$).
    \item Manual two's complement analysis of $x = -9$, inspecting the raw 32-bit binary pattern printed via \texttt{Serial.println((uint32\_t)x, BIN)}.
\end{enumerate}
